1. ENERGY BOUNDEDNESS
================================================================================

Every computation in this system is physically constrained by energy
availability. The neuromorphic core cannot operate beyond its energy
budget. This is not a software limit or a configuration parameter — it
is a physical consequence of the architecture.

The energy harvesting subsystem converts mechanical work from the motor
actuator into electrical energy through piezoelectric transduction. A
rectifier bridge converts the AC piezo output to DC, which charges a
supercapacitor. This stored energy powers the spiking neural network.

The energy governor enforces an activity-dependent cost model:

    E_cost = base_consumption + activity_cost x (spike_count / N)^2

This quadratic cost function means that high neural activity is
disproportionately expensive. The system naturally gravitates toward
moderate activity levels where energy harvest exceeds energy cost —
the self-sustaining operating region.

When energy reserves drop below critical thresholds, the energy governor
damps neural activity directly:

    Energy < 15 mWh:  modulation = -0.4  (maximum conservation)
    Energy < 30 mWh:  modulation = -0.1  (cautious reduction)
    Energy 30-80 mWh: modulation =  0.0  (neutral operation)
    Energy > 80 mWh:  modulation = +0.3  (surplus utilization)

This creates a homeostatic regulation loop analogous to biological
metabolic regulation. The system cannot "think harder than it can
afford." All neural computation is bounded by physical energy reality.

Validation evidence:

    Control condition (EnergyConfig):
    - base_consumption = 470 mW, friction_factor = 0.0005
    - Result: energy drains to -4,697 mWh after 2000 steps
    - The system FAILS to self-sustain (intentional null hypothesis)

    Experimental condition (BalancedEnergyConfig):
    - base_consumption = 45 mW, friction_factor = 18.0
    - Result: energy grows to +4,170 mWh after 2000 steps
    - The system SUCCEEDS at self-sustaining operation

    The comparison between these two configurations constitutes the
    falsifiable proof that the energy-bounded loop is achievable with
    appropriate physical parameters.

2. RECURSIVE REFLECTION
================================================================================

The recursive reflection module feeds the neuromorphic core's aggregate
output at timestep t back as additional input at timestep t+1:

    I_reflection(t+1) = previous_output(t) x reflection_coeff

Where:
    previous_output(t) = mean(V_1(t), V_2(t), ..., V_N(t))
    reflection_coeff   = base_coeff + modulation x sensitivity
    base_coeff         = 0.2 (default)
    sensitivity        = 0.1 (modulation gain)

This creates a one-step-delayed self-referential input channel. The
network receives, at each timestep, both the current external stimulus
and a scaled version of its own prior aggregate state.

The reflection coefficient is the system's operating parameter for
self-referential processing:

    reflection_coeff = 0.0:  No self-reference. Pure feedforward.
    reflection_coeff = 0.2:  Baseline. Subtle state-dependent bias.
    reflection_coeff = 0.3:  Enhanced. Prior state meaningfully shapes
                             current processing.
    reflection_coeff > 0.5:  Self-dominated. Internal state overwhelms
                             external input. (Not recommended for
                             operational deployment.)

The reflection module and the energy governor are bidirectionally
coupled. This is architecturally significant:

    Energy state → modulates reflection gain:
        Low energy reduces reflection coefficient (conserve resources)
        High energy increases reflection coefficient (explore/learn)

    Reflection-driven activity → influences energy cost:
        Higher reflection gain may increase spike activity
        Increased spike activity increases quadratic energy cost
        Increased energy cost triggers governor damping

This bidirectional coupling creates a self-regulating dynamic where the
system's degree of self-referential processing is automatically tuned
by its energy reality. The system cannot sustain arbitrarily high
self-reference — energy physics constrains it.

When combined with STDP (Spike-Timing Dependent Plasticity), the
reflection signal participates in synaptic learning:

    - The reflection input contributes to whether neurons fire
    - Firing patterns determine which synapses are strengthened (LTP)
      or weakened (LTD) via STDP
    - Over time, the network develops internal weight structures that
      are shaped by its own prior processing patterns
    - This is measurable: STDP with reflection achieves 6.52x higher
      mutual information than frozen-weight control (Claim F8.2)

3. PHYSICAL CLOSURE
================================================================================

The system forms four distinct closed loops, all grounded in physical
interaction with the environment:

Loop A — Energy Closure:
    The motor actuator performs physical work. That work generates
    mechanical energy (vibration, friction) on the piezoelectric
    element. The piezo converts mechanical energy to electrical energy.
    The electrical energy powers the neural network that commanded the
    motor activity in the first place. The loop is physically closed:
    neural computation → motor action → energy generation → neural
    computation.

Loop B — Reflection Closure:
    The neural network computes. Its aggregate output is recorded. That
    recorded output is fed back as input at the next timestep. The
    network processes its own prior state alongside new external input.
    The loop is temporally closed: computation(t) → observation(t) →
    input(t+1) → computation(t+1).

Loop C — Sensory Closure:
    The motor actuator perturbs the physical environment. The sensors
    detect changes in the environment. The sensor signals enter the
    neural network. The network drives the motor. The loop is
    environmentally closed: action → environmental change → perception
    → neural processing → action.

Loop D — Regulation Closure:
    Energy harvesting feeds the energy governor. The governor modulates
    the reflection coefficient and activity level. Modulated activity
    changes energy consumption. Changed consumption affects energy
    reserves. Changed reserves feed back to the governor. The loop is
    regulatorily closed: energy state → activity constraint → energy
    cost → energy state.

None of these loops are abstract or simulated-only. Each one
corresponds to a physical signal path that can be measured with
standard instrumentation:

    Loop A: Multimeter on supercapacitor voltage
    Loop B: ADC reading of membrane potential mean
    Loop C: Sensor readings before and after actuation
    Loop D: Energy storage level over time

The hardware reference design (Raspberry Pi Pico, SG90 servo, 27mm
piezo disc, BH1750 + DS18B20 sensors, total BOM < $25) implements all
four loops in a physically deployable form factor.

DRAWING DESCRIPTIONS — PATENT A

Self-Sustaining Neural-Motor Energy Harvesting Loop for Autonomous Cognitive Systems


BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS

FIG. 1 is a system architecture block diagram showing the self-sustaining
neural-motor energy harvesting loop with sensor, neural network, motor,
energy harvesting, and control subsystems interconnected in a closed loop.

FIG. 2 is a comparative energy balance chart showing control system energy
depletion versus experimental system energy growth over 2000 operational steps.

FIG. 3 is a schematic diagram of the spiking neural network architecture
showing the input layer, weight matrix, hidden layer with leaky
integrate-and-fire neurons, output stage, membrane potential dynamics, spike
vector output, and STDP learning rule.

FIG. 4 is a circuit schematic of the energy harvesting subsystem comprising
a piezoelectric transducer path with inductor, rectifier, and capacitor, and
a thermoelectric transducer path with heat sink, TEG module, and boost
converter, both feeding through a regulator to the SNN.

FIG. 5 is a graph showing the activity-dependent energy dynamics with harvest
and cost curves, crossover point, net positive region, energy trajectory, and
activity state pipeline.

FIG. 6 is a hardware reference design layout diagram showing the
microcontroller, piezo disc, servo motor, thermistor, photoresistor, LED
indicator, AC signal input, control logic, and energy storage components with
their interconnections.

FIG. 7 is a validation results summary comprising a claims validation table,
an energy comparison table, an energy trajectory graph, a legend, and a
summary statement.

FIG. 8 is a reference architecture diagram of the energy-bounded recursive
control system showing the signal flow from environment through sensor
interface, cognitive modulation, SNN core, motor output, thermal harvester,
ADC, piezo disc, and energy store, with reflection feedback.


DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS

Reference Numeral Convention: Each figure in this patent uses a per-figure
numbering series (FIG. 1 uses 100-series, FIG. 2 uses 200-series, through
FIG. 8 uses 800-series), with even-numbered increments. Where the same
physical component appears in multiple figures, it receives the numeral
appropriate to that figure's series.


FIG. 1 — System Architecture Block Diagram

FIG. 1 is a system architecture block diagram depicting the self-sustaining
neural-motor energy harvesting loop. The entire system operates within an
environment boundary 100, shown as a dashed rectangle.

Reference numerals: 100, 102, 104, 106, 108, 110, 112, 114, 116, 118, 120

A sensor 102 is depicted as a circle with a crosshair symbol, positioned at
the upper left of the diagram. The sensor 102 receives environmental input
and converts it to an electrical signal. An arrow labeled "input" connects
the sensor 102 to a spiking neural network 104.

The spiking neural network 104 is depicted as a bold-bordered rectangle
containing five interconnected neurons (small circles connected by lines),
labeled "SNN (LIF Neurons)." The spiking neural network 104 processes the
sensor input through a population of leaky integrate-and-fire neurons.

An arrow labeled "spikes" connects the spiking neural network 104 to a motor
106 depicted as a rectangle containing a motor arc symbol. The motor 106
converts neural output into mechanical actuation.

An arrow labeled "vibration" connects the motor 106 downward to a
piezoelectric element 112, depicted as a double circle labeled "PIEZO 27mm."
The piezoelectric element 112 converts mechanical vibration from the motor 106
into electrical energy.

An arrow labeled "heat" connects the spiking neural network 104 downward to a
thermal harvester 110, depicted as a rectangle containing a circle, labeled
"THERMAL." The thermal harvester 110 captures thermal energy generated during
neural computation.

Both the piezoelectric element 112 and the thermal harvester 110 connect via
arrows labeled "energy" to a control logic block 116, depicted as a diamond
shape labeled "CTRL LOGIC." The control logic block 116 manages the
distribution and regulation of harvested energy.

An energy store 118 is depicted as a rectangle labeled "ENERGY STORE,"
positioned adjacent to the control logic block 116. An arrow connects the
control logic block 116 to the energy store 118 for energy buffering.

A power management block 108 is depicted as a rectangle labeled "POWER MGT,"
positioned at the left. An arrow labeled "power" connects the power management
block 108 upward to the spiking neural network 104, delivering harvested
energy to sustain neural computation.

A ground reference 114 is depicted as a ground symbol labeled "GND,"
positioned at the right side of the diagram.

A reflection feedback path 120, shown as a dashed line, connects from the
control logic block 116 upward and back to the spiking neural network 104.
The reflection feedback path 120 represents the recursive self-observation
signal by which the system's prior computational output re-enters the neural
network as input on the next processing step.

A legend box at the bottom of the figure distinguishes three signal pathways:
solid arrows for main signal flow, long-dashed arrows for reflection feedback
(L8), and short-dashed arrows for ground reference. An annotation reads:
"THE LOOP CLOSES: SNN output drives motor, motor generates energy, energy
powers SNN."


FIG. 2 — Energy Balance Comparison

FIG. 2 is a comparative graph showing the energy trajectories of the control
and experimental system configurations over 2000 operational steps.

Reference numerals: 200, 202, 204, 206, 208, 210, 212, 214, 216, 218

The y-axis 200 represents cumulative energy in milliwatt-hours (mWh). The
x-axis 202 represents operational steps from 0 to 2000.

An experimental curve 204, drawn as a solid bold line, represents the
BalancedEnergyConfig (MCP path). The experimental curve 204 starts at the
origin and rises to the storage capacity ceiling, demonstrating that the
system achieves homeostatic equilibrium. A label 210 identifies this curve
near the upper right.

A control curve 212, drawn as a dashed line, represents the EnergyConfig (CLI
path). The control curve 212 starts at the origin and descends steadily,
reaching -4,697 mWh at step 2000, demonstrating that the control system
depletes all stored energy. A label 218 identifies this curve near the lower
right.

An origin point 206 is marked at the intersection of the axes with a circle,
representing the shared starting energy of both configurations.

A zero energy baseline 208 is shown as a horizontal dashed line at y=0,
serving as the self-sustaining threshold. Any trajectory above this line
indicates positive energy balance.

An experimental legend box 214 at the lower left shows the solid line style
with the label "Experimental (MCP)" and the value "+100.0 mWh @ 2000."

A control legend box 216 at the lower right shows the dashed line style with
the label "Control (CLI)" and the value "-4697 mWh @ 2000."


FIG. 3 — SNN Architecture (LIF Neuron Model)

FIG. 3 is a schematic diagram showing the internal structure of the spiking
neural network. The entire architecture is enclosed within a boundary
rectangle 300 labeled "SNN ARCHITECTURE (LIF Model)."

Reference numerals: 300, 302, 304, 306, 308, 310, 312, 314, 318, 320

An input layer 302 is depicted as a rectangle at the left containing four
circles. Two upper circles are labeled "ext" (external input) and "ref"
(reflection input), and two lower circles represent additional input channels.
The input layer 302 receives both the external sensor signal and the recursive
self-observation signal.

A weight matrix 304 is depicted as a rectangle containing a grid pattern
labeled "W[i,j]." The weight matrix 304 represents the all-to-all synaptic
connections between neurons, with each cell containing a trainable weight
value bounded to [0.0, 0.5].

A hidden layer 306 is depicted as a rectangle containing three neuron circles
at the top, with the center neuron labeled "i." Below the neurons, a neuron
detail section 308 displays the leaky integrate-and-fire equation:
"V(t+1) = V(t) x leak + I," the firing condition "If V >= V_th: spike," and
the threshold value "V_th = 0.5."

An output stage 310 is depicted as a rectangle containing a triangle (gate
symbol) labeled "OUTPUT." The output stage 310 generates discrete spike
events. An arrow from the output stage 310 exits to the right as the spike
output 312, representing the binary firing signal from each neuron.

A membrane potential dynamics display 314 is depicted as a dashed-bordered
rectangle below the main processing elements, containing a time-series
waveform of membrane potential oscillations around the threshold line V_th.
The display 314 shows the characteristic sub-threshold oscillation and
spike-reset dynamics of the LIF model.

A spike vector output 318 is depicted as a rectangle at the bottom of the
figure containing a bar graph representation of spike counts across all N
neurons. The label "spike_vector[N]" identifies the output format.

An STDP learning rule box 320 is depicted as a rectangle containing the
plasticity equations: "If post fires: W += A+ x pre_trace" (LTP),
"If pre fires: W -= A- x post_trace" (LTD), "A- > A+ (stability bias),"
and "Weight bounds: [0.0, 0.5]."

Arrows connect the elements in processing order: input layer 302 feeds into
weight matrix 304, which feeds into hidden layer 306, which feeds into output
stage 310. The output stage 310 connects to the spike vector output 318.


FIG. 4 — Energy Harvesting Circuit

FIG. 4 is an electrical circuit schematic showing the energy harvesting
subsystem. The entire circuit is enclosed within a dashed boundary rectangle
400.

Reference numerals: 400, 402, 404, 406, 408, 410, 412, 414, 416, 418, 420

The piezoelectric path occupies the upper portion of the circuit. A
piezoelectric disc 402 is depicted as a double circle representing the 27mm
piezo element. An AC signal 404 is depicted as a wavy line representing the
alternating voltage output from the piezoelectric disc 402. An inductor 406
is depicted as a rectangle containing a coil pattern, representing the LC
resonance circuit that amplifies the piezoelectric voltage. The LC resonance
provides 3-10x voltage amplification at the resonant frequency. A rectifier
408 is depicted as a diamond shape, representing the full-bridge rectifier
that converts AC to DC. A capacitor 410 is depicted as parallel plates,
representing the smoothing capacitor that filters the rectified output.

The thermoelectric path occupies the lower portion of the circuit. A heat
sink 412 is depicted as a finned rectangle. A thermoelectric module 414 is
depicted as a rectangle labeled "TEG" (thermoelectric generator). A boost
converter 416 is depicted as a rectangle containing a circle with a plus
symbol, representing the voltage step-up circuit.

Both paths converge at an output regulator 418 depicted as a rectangle
labeled "REG," which stabilizes the combined harvested voltage.

The regulated output feeds into a system output 420, depicted as a dashed
rectangle labeled "To SNN (104)," representing the energy delivery path to
the spiking neural network.

Signal flow arrows connect: 402 to 404 to 406 to 408 to 410 to 418
(piezoelectric path), 412 to 414 to 416 to 418 (thermoelectric path), and
418 to 420 (output). A dashed feedback arrow from the rectifier 408 returns
to the inductor 406.


FIG. 5 — Activity-Dependent Energy Dynamics

FIG. 5 is a graph showing the relationship between neural activity and energy
balance, derived from BalancedEnergyConfig parameters.

Reference numerals: 500, 502, 504, 506, 508, 510, 512, 514, 516

A main graph 500 occupies the upper portion of the figure, with axes labeled
"Energy (mWh)" on the y-axis and "Neural Activity (Spikes/Step)" on the
x-axis ranging from 0 to 50.

A harvest curve 502 is drawn as a solid bold line, gently increasing from
left to right. The harvest curve 502 represents the energy generated per step
as a function of neural activity, following the formula:
Harvest = 0.20 + (0.09 x spikes) mWh/step.

A cost curve 504 is drawn as a dashed line, rising more steeply than the
harvest curve 502 due to the quadratic activity penalty. The cost curve 504
represents the energy consumed per step, following the formula:
Cost = 0.225 + (0.006 x s) + (0.0003 x s^2) mWh/step.

A crossover point 506 is marked with a circle at approximately 0.3 spikes,
where the harvest curve 502 and cost curve 504 intersect. Below the crossover
point 506 (0 to 0.3 spikes), cost exceeds harvest (quiescent drain). A
quiescent drain zone label 508 identifies this region.

A sweet spot region 510 identifies the optimal activity range where the
margin between harvest and cost is greatest.

A net positive region 512 is depicted as a dashed rectangle enclosing the
area between curves where harvest exceeds cost, labeled "NET POSITIVE REGION:
Harvest > Cost."

An energy trajectory sub-graph 514 occupies the lower left, showing two
curves: a solid rising line (experimental) and a dashed falling line
(control) plotted against steps on the x-axis and energy on the y-axis.

An activity state pipeline 516 occupies the lower right, showing three
processing stages (MEASURE, COMPUTE, ADJUST) connected by arrows, with a
dashed feedback loop from ADJUST back to MEASURE.

A formula box at the bottom contains the exact energy formulas for harvest,
cost, and net energy.

FIG. 8 — Energy-Bounded Recursive Control Architecture

FIG. 8 is the canonical reference architecture diagram for the complete
energy-bounded recursive control system. The entire system is enclosed within
a dashed system boundary 800.

Reference numerals: 800, 802, 804, 806, 808, 810, 812, 814, 816, 818, 820

An environment input arrow 802 enters from the top of the diagram, labeled
"ENVIRONMENT," delivering environmental signals (light, temperature, force)
into the system.

A sensor interface 804 is depicted as a rectangle containing two sensor
circles, labeled "SENSOR INTERFACE." The sensor interface 804 receives the
environmental input and converts physical signals to electrical measurements.

A cognitive modulation block 806 is depicted as a cloud-like dashed shape
labeled "COGNITIVE," with internal label "mod." The cognitive modulation
block 806 represents the optional external cognitive control layer that
provides attention weighting and intention bias to the system.

A spiking neural network core 808 is depicted as a bold-bordered rectangle
containing three interconnected neuron circles, labeled "SNN CORE." The SNN
core 808 is the central processing element that executes leaky
integrate-and-fire neural dynamics.

A motor output 810 is depicted as a rectangle containing a feedback loop
symbol, labeled "MOTOR OUTPUT." The motor output 810 converts neural spike
patterns into mechanical actuation commands.

A thermal harvester 812 is depicted as a rectangle containing a circle
labeled "T," labeled "THERMAL HARVESTER." The thermal harvester 812 captures
heat energy generated by system operation.

An analog-to-digital converter 814 is depicted as a small rectangle
containing a circle, labeled "ADC." The ADC 814 digitizes analog harvested
signals for processing.

A piezoelectric disc 816 is depicted as a double circle, labeled "PIEZO
DISC." The piezoelectric disc 816 converts mechanical vibration into
electrical energy.

An energy store 818 is depicted as a rectangular capacitor symbol with
internal parallel plates, labeled "ENERGY STORE." The energy store 818
buffers harvested energy for sustained operation.

A reflection block 820 is depicted as a dashed-bordered rectangle containing
the equation "r(t) = y(t-1)," labeled "Reflection." The reflection block 820
implements recursive self-observation by feeding the prior neural output back
as input on the next timestep.

Signal flow: The environment input 802 feeds into the sensor interface 804.
The sensor interface 804 feeds rightward into the cognitive modulation
block 806, which feeds into the SNN core 808, which feeds into the motor
output 810. Below, the thermal harvester 812 feeds into the ADC 814, which
feeds into the piezoelectric disc 816, which feeds into the energy store 818.

A bold dashed energy sustaining loop connects from the energy store 818 along
the bottom and right side back to the thermal harvester 812, representing the
energy recycling path. A dashed reflection feedback path connects from the
reflection block 820 upward and rightward back to the SNN core 808.

A dashed cognitive modulation path connects from the cognitive modulation
block 806 to the SNN core 808.

The thermal harvester 812 connects downward to the reflection block 820.

A legend box at the bottom of the figure distinguishes four signal pathway
styles: solid arrows for main signal flow, long dashes for energy sustaining
loop, medium dashes for reflection feedback, and short dashes for cognitive
modulation.
